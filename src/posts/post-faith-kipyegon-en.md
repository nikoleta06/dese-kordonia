**Faith Kipyegon** has been at the top of the 1500m for more than ten years. Lasting that long in such a fast event is rare. To understand how she did it, it's worth looking at her path in order.

### The early years

<ul class="career-timeline">
  <li><span class="year">1994</span>Born in Kenya. As a child she runs kilometres every day just to get to school.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2010</span>At 16 she becomes world junior cross-country champion.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2011</span>World youth champion in the 1500m.</li>
</ul>

The talent showed early. But plenty of kids win medals at 16 and then disappear. What came next, in her case, was slower.

### The top

<ul class="career-timeline">
  <li><span class="year">2015</span>Silver at the World Championships in Beijing, behind Genzebe Dibaba.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2016</span>First Olympic gold in the 1500m, in Rio.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2017</span>World champion in London.</li>
</ul>

### The pause

<ul class="career-timeline">
  <li><span class="year">2018</span>Her daughter Alyn is born. She misses the whole racing season.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2019</span>She comes back and takes silver at the World Championships in Doha.</li>
</ul>

Kipyegon just needed a little time to find her rhythm again.

### Stronger than before

<ul class="career-timeline">
  <li><span class="year">2021</span>Second Olympic gold in the 1500m, in Tokyo, in an Olympic record.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2022</span>World champion again, in Eugene.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2023</span>Within a few weeks she breaks three world records: 1500m, 5000m and the mile. At the World Championships in Budapest she wins gold at both distances.</li>
  <li><span class="year">2024</span>A third straight Olympic gold in the 1500m, in Paris. No one before her, man or woman, had done it.</li>
</ul>

### What I see in this list

Read it from top to bottom and it's hard to find a single year when everything changed. You see silvers before the golds, a year without racing and a lot of work in between. That's how our own progress comes too: one training cycle at a time.

Try writing your own list, from the day you started running. Your first run without stopping, your first race, your first break. You'll see you've come further than you think.
