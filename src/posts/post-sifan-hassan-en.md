Tokyo, 2 August 2021. A 1500m heat at the Olympic Games. On the final lap, a runner in front of her trips and **Sifan Hassan** goes down with her. By the time she gets up, the pack is already well ahead.

Then she starts running. Over the last 200 metres she passes her rivals one by one and finishes **first**. That same evening she runs the 5000m final and wins gold.

By the end of those Games she had three medals: gold in the 5000m and 10,000m, bronze in the 1500m. Three different distances, in one week.

### From Ethiopia to the Netherlands

Sifan was born in Ethiopia in 1993 and arrived in the Netherlands at 15, as a refugee. That's where she started running, and within a few years she was at the top of the world in the 1500m. She could have stayed there, at the distance she knew well. She chose to keep moving up: 5000m, 10,000m and, in the end, the marathon.

### London, 2023: her first marathon

She ran her first marathon while fasting for Ramadan. Around the middle of the race she stopped twice to stretch her hip while the others pulled away. Many thought her race was over. She caught them one by one and won in the sprint.

She has admitted many times that she feels fear and doubt before a big race. She still shows up at the start line, every time.

### Paris, 2024

At the Paris Olympics she did something that seems almost impossible: she ran the 5000m, the 10,000m and the marathon at the same Games. She took bronze in the first two. On the final day she won **gold in the marathon**, in an Olympic record, in a duel decided in the last few metres.

### What I take from her story

We all fall at some point. In a bad workout, an injury, a stretch when nothing goes the way we wanted. Sifan shows us what you do right after: you get up and keep going, even if the pack is far ahead.
